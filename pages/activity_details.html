Poster Insights Session

Explore research posters on Free Play, share key insights with peers, and engage in a facilitated Q&A to deepen understanding of play-based learning in kindergarten settings.

Objectives

  • Explore research posters on Free Play practices
  • Surface key insights and takeaways from posters
  • Engage in facilitated Q&A and discussion
  • Connect poster content to classroom experience

Setup

Plan A / B / C / D (TBC). Posters are displayed around the venue. Participants move freely or are guided through poster stations.

Step 1: Poster Reading

10 mins
Participant Actions
  • Browse posters individually or in pairs
  • Note findings or questions that stand out
Facilitator Actions
  1. Introduce the poster circuit and navigation instructions
  2. Encourage active reading and jotting down impressions

Step 2: Group Discussion on Posters

10 mins
Participant Actions
  • Gather in small groups to share what resonated
  • Discuss connections to your own classroom practice
Facilitator Actions
  1. Prompt groups with guiding questions (e.g., "What surprised you?")
  2. Circulate and listen for themes to surface in Q&A

Step 3: Interactive Q&A

20 mins
Participant Actions
  • Raise questions or reflections with poster presenters or facilitators
  • Share insights that could inform your own practice
Facilitator Actions
  1. Open floor for questions, directing to appropriate poster presenters
  2. Synthesize key themes emerging from the discussion
  3. Close with a brief bridge to Activity 2

"Our Playful Community" Resource Mapping

Collaboratively identify and evaluate local community resources through the lens of quality play principles to strengthen the home-school-community partnership.

Interactive Padlet Board

Use this board to map community resources

Open Padlet

Objectives

  • Evaluate spaces using CPS playfulness dimensions
  • Co-create a digital map of community resources
  • Spot opportunities compensating for urban play limitations
  • Generate practical parent takeaways

Grouping Strategy

Pre-assigned small groups. Participants stay with their assigned table groups; when posting to Padlet, they should select the relevant district section for the location being shared.

Step 1.0: Introduction

2 mins
Participant Actions
  1. Scan QR code to access the Padlet board
  2. Stay with the pre-assigned group and enter the correct Padlet district section for the places being mapped
Facilitator Actions
  1. Clearly state goal: create collaborative map
  2. Introduce Padlet and the 4-step process

Step 2.0: Community Brainstorming

10 mins
Participant Actions
  1. Work within the pre-assigned group
  2. Brainstorm local spaces (parks, libraries, waterfronts)
  3. Post each location as a new item on the Padlet map
  4. Fill in the custom fields: Select the District (區段) and Location Type (地方類別)
  5. Complete the post details: Add the district section, location type, and any recommendation notes you want to keep with the map entry
Facilitator Actions
  1. Circulate and challenge groups to look beyond standard playgrounds
  2. Prompt for unique spaces (water, sand, messy play) addressing urban limitations

Step 3.0: Analysis & Annotation

10 mins
Participant Actions
  1. Use "Padlet Usage Guide" to review how locations were mapped and documented
  2. Add comments to Padlet evaluating space against prompt sheet principles
Facilitator Actions
  • Prompt with targeted questions: "How to use storytelling here?" or "How to empower parents as facilitators?"
Materials: Padlet Usage Guide CPS 向度提示卡 (CPS Prompt Sheet)

Step 4.0: Synthesis Preparation

3 mins
Participant Actions
  1. Review annotated map
  2. Identify 1-2 key landmarks or surprising insights
  3. Designate member to share takeaway in under 2 mins
Facilitator Actions
  • Announce transition. Remind groups to be concise and focus on most impactful finding.

Step 5.0: Whole-Group Share-Back

15 mins
Participant Actions
  • One representative from each group shares their key takeaway with the entire audience.
Facilitator Actions
  • Facilitate brief wrap-up, highlighting common themes or innovative ideas across districts.
  • Note: Select five groups for stand-up sharing (one per district)

Required Materials

  • Posters displayed around venue (pre-set before session)
  • Visible timer / clock
  • Sticky notes or index cards (for participant jottings, optional)
  • Pens / pencils per participant

Facilitation Tips

  • Poster Reading: Encourage participants to move at their own pace and jot down questions or observations as they browse.
  • Group Discussion: Use open prompts — "What stood out?" / "What connects to your own class?"
  • Q&A: Direct specific questions to poster presenters; synthesise emerging themes before closing.
  • Bridge to Activity 2 by noting how poster insights can inform community resource evaluation.

Tech & Materials Checklist

Device per group: 1 iPad/tablet/phone
Internet: Venue Wi-Fi + portable hotspots
Padlet Pack: Printed QR + short URL + instructions
Padlet Usage Guide: Print here
Table cards: Big district labels ("Tuen Mun / Yuen Long / …")
Charging: Extension cords + power banks
Stationery: Marker pens + sticky notes
Privacy Card: "No child photos / personal data"

Two "Reality Checks"

Apply these during evaluation:

1. Feasibility for families
Accessible, low-cost, realistic? (time, transport, weather)
2. Managed risk & etiquette
Boundaries that keep kids safe without switching off playfulness?

Post-Activity Showcase

Post-event, the KeySteps Team migrates Padlet data to Google Maps to create a permanent, shareable guide.

Transferable Learning: Use map in debriefing to discuss how teachers will engage parents and advocate for community play.
